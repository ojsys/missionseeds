# Mission Seedlings — Website Guide for Staff

Everything you need to run the website day to day. No technical knowledge assumed.

**Website:** https://missionseedlings.com
**Sign in:** https://missionseedlings.com/admin/login.php

---

## 1. Signing in

Go to the sign-in page and enter the username and password you were given.

If it is your first time, you will be asked to choose your own password straight away. Pick
something at least 10 characters long — a short phrase you will remember beats a short scramble
you won't.

**If you forget your password,** ask a Super Admin to reset it. They will give you a new
temporary one and you will choose your own again on the next sign-in.

**Five wrong password attempts** locks the account for 15 minutes. That is deliberate — it stops
anyone guessing their way in. Wait it out and try again.

You are signed out automatically after 30 minutes of inactivity.

**Partner organisations do not get accounts.** They follow progress on the public Growth Tracker,
and when they ask for a fuller update, a Project Manager sends one (see section 8).

## 3. Editing words on the website

The quickest way to change text is to **edit it where you see it**.

1. Sign in.
2. Open any page of the public website.
3. Hover over a headline or paragraph — a soft gold outline appears.
4. Click it, type your change, then click somewhere else.
5. A brief green flash means it saved. There is no "save" button.

This works for headings, intros, and body paragraphs across every page.

Things that are **not** edited this way — because they are dates, links, or numbers that the site
has to understand rather than just display — live in **Settings** or their own admin screen.

## 4. Keeping the Growth Tracker up to date

**Admin → Indicators.**

The everyday job: type the new numbers into the "Update the figures" table and press
**Save figures** once. Every change is dated automatically, which is what draws the little trend
lines on the public page.

If your church records are already up to date, press **Fill from church records** instead and the
totals are copied across for you.

To hide an indicator from the public, or add a new one, use the form below the table.

> **Never add an indicator for money.** No budget, no seed-fund balances, no allowances. This table
> feeds the public page, and the project's privacy commitment says financial data stays private.

---

## 5. Churches

**Admin → Churches.**

Add a church, then keep its stage, participant count, seedlings, trees, and survival rate current.
A profile stays invisible to the public until you tick **Publish this profile**.

> **Never type personal data into a church record.** No participant names, phone numbers, household
> income, ID numbers, or home locations. Those belong in KoboToolbox. Everything on this screen can
> appear on the public website.

The latitude and longitude fields are for the **church building**, for a future map. They are never
shown publicly, and they must never be a member's home.

## 6. Stories and photos

**Admin → Stories.**

Write an update, choose its category and church, add a cover photo, and set the status:

- **Draft** — still working on it, nobody sees it.
- **Awaiting review** — a coordinator has sent it in and it needs a decision.
- **Published** — live on the website.
- **Needs changes** — sent back to the author with a note.

When a coordinator submits something, a yellow bar appears on your dashboard. Open the story list,
read it, and either **Approve & publish** or **Send back** with a note explaining what to change.

### Photo consent

Before publishing a photo where someone is recognisable, make sure they have agreed to it being
used. Tick the consent box only when they have. Photos without consent stay in the library but are
flagged everywhere they appear, so nobody publishes one by accident.

**Admin → Media** is where you upload photos, add descriptions, and record consent.

Always write a description for each photo — it is what a blind visitor's screen reader reads aloud.

## 7. Resources and KoboToolbox

**Admin → Resources.**

Every resource has a visibility setting:

- **Public** — anyone visiting the website.
- **Church coordinators and staff** — needs a coordinator or staff sign-in.
- **Project staff only** — the most restricted.

Restricted resources are not merely hidden; they are never sent to a browser that is not allowed to
see them.

**KoboToolbox forms** are managed at the bottom of the same page. Paste the shareable form link
from Kobo and choose who can open it. Responses stay in KoboToolbox — this website only links to
the form and never stores what people submit.

## 8. Sending a progress update to a partner

Partner organisations do not have logins. When one asks how the project is going:

1. Open https://missionseedlings.com/tracker
2. Press **Ctrl + P** (or **Cmd + P** on a Mac) and choose **Save as PDF**.
3. Email them the PDF.

The page is styled to print cleanly: current figures, project phase, and the milestone timeline,
with the website navigation stripped out.

## 9. Enquiries

**Admin → Enquiries.** Messages from the website's contact form.

Mark each one **Read**, **Responded**, or **Archived** as you deal with it, and add an internal
note if a colleague needs the context.

> This page holds people's names, emails, and phone numbers. They gave them to us so we could
> reply — use them for that and nothing else, don't copy them anywhere else, and delete enquiries
> once they are genuinely finished with.

## 10. Running a new call for applications

The call-for-applications page lives permanently at **/call**. You do not rebuild it each time.

**To open a new call:**

1. **Admin → Settings → Call for applications**
2. Set the new **deadline**
3. Paste the new **expression of interest form URL**
4. Set **Call status** to *Open*
5. Update the page wording by clicking it directly on /call

While the call is open, the main navigation button says "Apply now" and points at it.

**To close a call:** set the status to *Closed*. The page stays up with a "this call has closed"
notice, and the navigation button switches to "Express interest" pointing at the contact page. Set
it to *Hidden* to take the page down entirely.

---

## 11. Accounts

**Admin → Users** (Super Admins only).

To add someone: fill in a username, name, and role, then press Create. The system shows a
**temporary password once** — copy it and give it to them directly, by phone or WhatsApp. They must
choose their own password when they first sign in.

If it gets lost, just reset the password again. It cannot be looked up.

For a **church coordinator**, you must also choose their church. That is the only church they will
be able to post about.

**Deactivate rather than delete** when someone leaves. It blocks sign-in immediately but keeps the
record of what they did.

---

## 12. What must never go on the public website

The project's privacy commitment. Everyone with an account is responsible for it.

- Project budget, bank details, seed-fund balances, staff allowances
- Participant names and phone numbers
- Household income or household data
- National identification records
- Individual household GPS coordinates
- Internal cooperative account information

The website is deliberately built so most of these are impossible to enter — there is no field for
them anywhere. The rest depend on you: don't type them into a description, a story, or a photo
caption.

---

## 13. If something goes wrong

**A page shows an error.** Try again in a minute. If it persists, contact your developer with the
page address and roughly when it happened.

**Someone changed something and nobody knows who.** Admin → Activity shows every change, who made
it, and when.

**A photo won't upload.** It is probably too large — anything over 4MB is rejected. Compress it at
squoosh.app first. This also keeps the site fast for people on slow connections, which matters.

**You're locked out.** Wait 15 minutes, or ask another Super Admin to reset your password.
